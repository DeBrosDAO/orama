# What do I earn

What a validator, a storage provider and a relay earn per epoch under the chain's parameters, for a small and a large operator and a newcomer with no stake.

Every number in the tables below is computed from the chain's parameters by a test (`TestEarningsPage_tablesMatchTheParameters` in `chain/app`), and the table of sources at the end names the line of code that sets each input. The test fails when a parameter changes, so this page cannot drift from the code. The figures are what the rules allow, in ORAMA, before anything the market adds. They are not a price forecast and not a promise: nothing here says what an ORAMA is worth.

## How to read the tables

- **Epochs, not days.** Pay and the halving schedule count epochs. A mainnet epoch is 24 hours of block time and at least 14,400 blocks, so one a day. The stagenet installer uses 300 seconds, so 288 a day, and each stagenet epoch pays the same per-epoch amount. A stagenet therefore runs through the schedule 288 times faster.
- **Shares are ceilings.** Only the validator share is minted every epoch. Storage and relay are minted when a provider or relay is actually paid, never beyond the ceiling, and the development share only on a governance-approved spend. What nobody claims is never created. See [Supply and emission](/docs/blockchain/supply).
- **Everything lands in earnings.** Pay is credited to the operator's earnings account. The owner can withdraw it to a bank balance with `MsgWithdrawEarnings` and send it from there; see [Fees](/docs/blockchain/fees).
- **Storage and relay pay 90%.** Of each service payment, 90% goes to the operator, 5% is burned and 5% funds the archive.

## Validators

The validator share of an epoch is paid in full to the validators and their delegators, pro rata to voting power, whether there are 30 validators or 300. So what a validator earns is the pool times its share of power, and its share is its operator's share of bonded stake after the cap, split over that operator's validators. Fees do not pay validators: the base fee is burned, so after the halvings the pool is the whole income.

- **The large operator** holds 30% of all bonded stake but is held to the 5% cap per operator. The excess goes to the others, so each average operator in the example holds more than its raw 1.8%. Running more validators does not raise an operator's share; see [Validators and staking](/docs/blockchain/validators).
- **The small operator** gets a larger share than its raw 1% because the cap redistributes the excess pro rata.
- **A newcomer with no stake** earns nothing from the validator share. Power after the hand-over is stake power; a seat on the bootstrap committee exists only until lambda reaches 1. A newcomer who bonds stake earns on the part of it that has been admitted, and an increase ramps in over 30 epochs.
- The table gives what the validator and its delegators earn together. The validator keeps its commission and its own delegation's share and the rest goes to delegators. Commission has no cap in the code.

## Storage providers

A customer sets the price of a deal, and there is no minimum price. A provider is paid for each slot it proves, and at most the sample size (8) of a node's slots are challenged and paid in an epoch, so a node's income is bounded by the sample size times the prices of the deals it holds.

- **A small provider** holds one deal and one sampled slot.
- **A large provider** holds the full sample of 8 slots on one node. More nodes means more samples.
- **The subsidy** adds a fraction of the price on private deals, from the storage ceiling, once at least 8 distinct operators are active, rising to the full price at 32. It is capped per operator.
- **A newcomer with no stake** can register a probation node with no bond, which may take one protocol-deal slot. Protocol deals are scheduled only when the genesis sets `protocol_every_epochs`, and it is 0 in the defaults, so a probation node earns only from protocol deals a network turns on. A probation node that proves nothing in four epochs is jailed; one that proves graduates. See [Storage deals](/docs/blockchain/storage-deals).

## Relays

A relay's pay is the median of at least three reporters' measured bandwidth, in norama, so a relay needs no stake and no bond: a newcomer starts as a small relay. A relay is paid only while its node is registered and live, its median uptime is at least 0.9, and the first epoch with quorum only switches rewards on and pays nothing. An exit relay is paid twice its weight, within the per-operator cap. The per-relay and per-operator caps bound what one relay and one operator can take, and the sum of all relays is scaled down to the epoch's ceiling if it is larger.

Bandwidth measurement (`sbws`) is not built yet, so until it runs the reported weights are zero and no relay is paid. See [Relay rewards](/docs/blockchain/relay-rewards).
