# Validators and staking

Bonds live in `x/staking`. The voting power CometBFT uses is computed by `x/power` at the end of every block. Staking's own validator-set updates are discarded, so `x/power` is the only module that tells CometBFT who validates.

## Becoming a validator

An outsider creates a validator with the normal staking message (`MsgCreateValidator`) and delegators bond to it with `MsgDelegate`. Both can top up a bank shortfall from the signer's own earnings, inside the message and all or nothing.

| Staking and slashing setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Unbonding time | 21 days (1,814,400 seconds) |
| Maximum validators | 100 |
| Maximum unbonding entries | 7 |
| Minimum commission rate | 0 |
| Double-sign slash | 5% |
| Downtime slash | 0.01% |
| Signed-blocks window | 10,000, minimum 50% signed |
| Downtime jail | 600 seconds |
| Minimum delegation to earn rewards | 1 ORAMA |

The size of the active set (60 to 100) is not final: the benchmark that decides it has not run.

## Power is not raw stake

Each validator gets a power figure `P = (1 - lambda) x B + lambda x C`:

- `B` is the bootstrap share, `1/n` for each member of the bootstrap committee.
- `C` is the validator's share of bonded stake after the limits below.
- `lambda` slides from 0 to 1 as the network matures (next section).

The limits on `C`:

**A cap.** No validator's capped share can exceed 5% of the set. Once more than 60 validators are active, the cap becomes 3%. It returns to 5% only after the active count has stayed under 50 for 30 epochs. Power removed from a capped validator is redistributed by water-filling.

**A ramp.** A validator that has just become eligible does not receive its full share at once. The share ramps in over 30 epochs.

**A redistribution bound.** Power moved off a capped validator cannot lift another validator above twice that validator's own raw stake share (`max_redistribution_multiplier` = 2).

**A per-block limit.** Power increases are limited to one third (total variation) per block. Decreases apply immediately.

CometBFT receives `floor(P x 1e9)`, with a floor of 1 for any positive share. Jailed and tombstoned validators are excluded.

A "useful-work multiplier" parameter exists with default 1.0 and `activated = false`. Nothing reads it. It does not change power today. A governance proposal can activate it (see [Governance](/docs/blockchain/governance)), but nothing consumes the setting yet.

## The bootstrap committee and the hand-over

A new chain has no stake, so it starts with a *bootstrap committee*: validators declared in genesis (`oramad genesis add-bootstrap-validator`) who hold equal power without any stake. A production chain id requires at least 30 of them. A devnet, stagenet or localnet may have fewer.

`lambda` starts at 0 and only ever goes up. It is recomputed once per closed epoch:

```
lambda = min(1, max(lambda_previous,
                    bonded stake / 271,000 ORAMA,
                    epochs since genesis / 365))
```

So the committee fades out when bonded stake reaches 271,000 ORAMA or at epoch 365, whichever comes first. Two protections:

- Until the active validator count reaches a gate (twice the validators needed to fill 100% at the current cap), lambda is held at 0.95. Crossing the gate sets a flag permanently.
- Each step is shortened if needed so ramped shares and capped-stake shifts stay within one third per block.

At lambda = 1 a seat that exists only on the committee drops to zero power.

**Force-bonding.** While lambda is below 1, half of a committee member's reward is self-delegated into its own validator until the self-bond reaches twice the minimum (2 x 1,000 ORAMA = 2,000 ORAMA). A guard stops a committee member undelegating below that amount while lambda is below 1. Force-bonding is skipped if the member has no validator record yet, is jailed, or has an invalid exchange rate; the whole reward then stays in earnings, so one bad validator cannot halt an epoch.

## Rewards

At the end of an epoch `x/emission` mints the 60% validator share and `x/power` pays it on the same power figures CometBFT used. Rewards are paid on *capped* power, not on the stock distribution module.

Each validator's amount is split by its commission. The commission plus the operator's own self-delegation share goes to the operator. The rest is split across delegators in proportion to their shares. Everyone is credited in an earnings account. There is no withdraw transaction. A delegation smaller than 1 ORAMA is skipped.

## Slashing and jailing

Downtime is slashed 0.01% inside a window of 10,000 blocks. A double-sign is slashed 5% and tombstones the validator. Jailing and undelegation go through `x/staking`. Slashing applies to every CometBFT validator including bootstrap-committee seats.

A node that has a bonded role in `x/nodes` is separately slashed there when it fails a storage challenge. See [Nodes](/docs/blockchain/nodes).

## Operating a validator key

Moving a validator to a new machine without a double-sign is the most dangerous routine job on the chain. The CLI does it in steps (all under `orama maint global validator`):

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `export-key` | Writes `priv_validator_key.json` sealed to your public key |
| `reseal` | Turns a key backup into a migration bundle for a new host |
| `migrate prepare` | On the new host: prints the host's migration key |
| `migrate export` | On the old host: stops the chain and seals the key and its sign state |
| `migrate import` | On the new host: installs the key and records its sign floor |
| `migrate cancel` | Removes a prepared migration key |
| `check-sign-floor` | Fails when the chain must not start: the key moved away, or state is behind its floor |
| `unjail`, `edit` | Build or send `MsgUnjail` and `MsgEditValidator` |

The chain's systemd unit runs `check-sign-floor` before every start, so a stale copy of a key cannot sign. Full runbook: [Global nodes](/docs/operator/global-nodes#validators).

## Queries

`x/power` has no messages. Queries: `params`, `bootstrap-committee`, `lambda`, `validator-power` (only `comet_power` is filled) and `invariants`. `orama chain validator` lists the set. See [Reading the chain](/docs/blockchain/reading-the-chain).
